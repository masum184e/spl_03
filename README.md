### Thesis Proposal

<details> <summary>What Actually Happens in a Thesis Proposal?</summary>

We are instructed to submit our thesis proposal document, which will be reviewed and given feedback by the committee.

The proposal document must follow the provided template and should be prepared after discussing and reviewing the research topic and proposal with our supervisor.

Before submission, the proposal will go through Turnitin to check for plagiarism and AI generated content, as required by the department.

After the committee reviews the proposal, there are two possible outcomes:

- If the proposal is approved, we are good to proceed with the thesis.
- If the committee requires changes, we need to make the required revisions according to the committee's feedback and resubmit the revised proposal.

No presentation, or one to one viva happend here. Just submitted the documents in the office.

### Thesis Mid

<details>

<summary>What documents and progress do we need to prepare for the Mid term Defence?</summary>

**1. Thesis Report** *[Soft Copy]*

The thesis report should mainly include the Introduction section with the Motivation, Problem Statement, Research Question, and Contribution, along with a complete Literature Review.

**2. 1-Page Summary** *[Hard Copy + Supervisor's Signature]*

The summary should include the Project Title, Research Question, Implementation Pipeline, and a brief meeting/progress summary with dates for each meeting held with the supervisor from proposal submission to the mid-term.

**3. 1-Page Literature Summary** *[Soft Copy]*

The literature summary should contain at least 10 related works, covering their pipelines, methods, pros and cons, results, and relevant comments or observations.

**4. Code Base**

The code base should demonstrate the implementation progress according to the proposed project pipeline. **If a dataset is used**, its details and references to previous studies that used the dataset should also be provided.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What are the requirements and instructions for the Defence Mid?</summary>

The Mid term Defence Board list has been provided, and each student has to contact their respective teacher to know their defence schedule. The schedule will be decided by the respective board.

Each student must bring a laptop to show their project progress and necessary documents.

Students who do not have the supervisor's signature on the hard copy of the summary sheet will not be allowed to enter the board and will receive a penalty in the midterm.

No presentation happened here, but one to one viva conduct with respective teacher(ovbiously not your supervisor).
</details>

### Thesis Final Presentation

<details>
<summary>What is blind review?</summary>

Around **1–2 weeks before the defence**, the committee asks students to submit **two hard copies of the report** for blind review.

Only the **research title** should be visible. The **student's name and supervisor's name must be hidden**.

The report is then randomly assigned to **two committee members**, who read and review it carefully, often line by line.

The review may include:

- Formatting issues
- Incorrect or inappropriate methodology
- Mismatch between the abstract/introduction and methodology
- Inconsistencies between the methodology and results
- Whether the results actually support the desired outcome
- Other technical or structural issues in the report

This review is very important for your report. If the reviewers provide detailed and appropriate feedback, you can learn a lot from it and improve the quality of your thesis before the final defence.

> There have marks allocated on this blind review, so work on the copy properly, **don't** think it's just for review, **don't** just submit it somehow.

In defence, we were asked to bring the two blind-review copies, but they did not collect them.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What documents need to be submitted?</summary>

The main things you need to prepare are:

Soft Copy

- **Final report**
- **Presentation file** around **10–12 slides**

Hard Copy

- **Book-bound copies of the final report**
- **Approval pages** for board signatures

The general process is:

1. Submit the final report for **Turnitin and AI checking**.
2. After the checking/review process, print the final version of the report.
3. Get the report **book-bound**.
4. Make sure the book-bound report contains the **supervisor's signature**.
5. Submit the **report and presentation file before the defence day**, according to the department's instructions.
6. Bring the **book-bound report on the defence day**.
7. Bring **3 copies of the approval page** so that the board can sign them.

The signed approval pages may be needed later when preparing additional copies of the report for the **hall, teacher, department, or other purposes**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do we need to carry a laptop to show the progress or software?</summary>

Mostly, **no**.

However, it can be useful to have your laptop or software ready in case a board member wants to:

- See your software/application
- Check an output
- See a demonstration
- Verify something related to your implementation

So, although it may not be mandatory, having your project ready to demonstrate can be helpful.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do they check the report again?</summary>

Yes, but usually only to some extent.

In my case, the board members checked the report and found that I had used some **preprint papers as references**.

> **Preprint paper:** A research paper that has been made publicly available before completing the formal peer-review and publication process.

Board members are experts in their respective fields and have been doing research for years. By simply going through the pages of your report, they can often identify mistakes or potential issues.

They may notice things such as:

- Incorrect methodology
- Weak justification
- Inappropriate references
- Inconsistencies
- Problems with the experimental setup
- Unsupported claims
- Issues with the results or conclusion

They can also ask questions based on these observations.

So, don't assume that the board will only look at your presentation. They may already know the important details of your report.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do they check the report with Turnitin and the hard copy to find matches?</summary>

Mostly, **no**.

They generally don't manually compare the hard copy with the Turnitin report during the defence.

However, you should still make sure that your final report is properly checked and follows the required academic and formatting guidelines.

> Don't assume that something won't be checked. If there is an issue in your report, a board member may notice it while going through the report.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What actually happens on the defence day?</summary>

The defence schedule is usually provided beforehand.

In my case, my slot was **after lunch**. I had lunch and then went to the conference room with my classmates.

The board members were already conducting the defences of other students, so I waited until my turn.

When my turn came:

1. I gave my presentation slides to the computer operator.
2. I started my presentation.
3. I initially presented in **English**.
4. After around two slides, an external board member asked me about the **actual research gap**.
5. The question was asked in **Bangla**, so I answered in Bangla.
6. I continued the rest of my presentation in Bangla.

During the defence, they asked questions about:

- The research gap
- My two different deep learning architectures
- My contribution
- The fusion layer
- Why I used a weighted average
- How I determined the weights
- Whether there was a specific method for selecting those weights
- YOLO architecture
- My results
- What my research actually concluded

For the fusion weights, I explained that I had **run experiments and tested different weights**.

The board member acknowledged that I had experimented with them, but then asked whether I had followed any established method for determining the weights.

I said that I was **not aware of a specific method** for selecting the weights.

The entire defence took approximately **5–7 minutes** in my case.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does the board mainly focus on?</summary>

Based on my experience, the major areas of focus were:

- **Research gap**
- **Model architecture**
- **Results**
- **Research contribution**

You should be particularly prepared to explain:

- **Research Gap:** What problem exists in existing research, and what is missing that your research tries to address?
- **Model Architecture:** Why did you select the models? How do they work? Why did you use particular components?
- **Results:** What did you achieve? What metrics did you use? What do the results mean?
- **Contribution:** What did you actually contribute through this research?

Don't just describe what you implemented. Be prepared to explain **why you implemented it and what your research contributes**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How was the marking distributed?</summary>

The marking was divided into:

- **45% — Supervisor**
- **55% — Board**

However, the evaluation is not based only on a single presentation or the defence.

It depends on the **whole thesis process**, including:

- Mid progress
- Blind review
- Meeting with supervisor
- Contribution
- Presentation
- Overall performance throughout the process

So, don't focus only on preparing the presentation. Make sure you understand your **entire research work and report**.

</details>
